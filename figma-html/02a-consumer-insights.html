02A · CONSUMER INSIGHT HYPOTHESES · Bonus evidence
Pain points translated into product opportunities

The problem is not exchanging information. It is the friction that happens during and after the introduction.

These are hypotheses derived from the brief and category research, not claims from completed interviews. They are written in a form that can be validated later through usability testing and contextual interviews.

Moment → friction → need → response

Each insight connects a human moment to a concrete UX decision.

MomentLikely frictionConsumer insight hypothesisDesign implication
Meeting someoneConversation loses momentum while people install, search or type.“I want the exchange to feel like part of the conversation, not a setup task.”No-app recipient path; one share action; visible browser-first proof.
After receiving a paper cardCard can be lost; details still need to be typed into the phone.“If I have to re-enter the details later, I may never do it — or I may type them incorrectly.”Structured Save Contact outcome instead of OCR/retyping.
International networkingNames and characters may be unfamiliar or hard to reproduce.“Keep the identity exactly as the owner entered it; don’t make me transliterate it.”Preserve original-script contact data end to end.
Professional follow-upPhone, email and social handles live in different places.“I need one recognizable identity I can find again, not five disconnected usernames.”One profile model combining contact + selected social channels.

Two-sided experience

The sender and recipient have different success criteria.

Sender

“Help me look prepared without slowing the conversation.”

Success = fast share, professional identity, the right details in one place, confidence that the recipient can use them.

Recipient

“Help me understand who this is and keep the useful information.”

Success = no app requirement, clear identity, accurate save, familiar storage and easy future retrieval.

Validation next: interview and observe real networking/sales scenarios to learn which friction is most severe, then revise the hierarchy instead of treating these hypotheses as proven facts.
