02A · CONSUMER INSIGHTS · Hypotheses
Pain point → insight → design response

The exchange is social and time-sensitive. Every extra setup step competes with the conversation.

These are consumer insight hypotheses inferred from the brief and category behavior. They are not presented as completed interviews. I use them to explain why each marketing section exists and what friction it is meant to reduce.

Four moments of friction

Each hypothesis connects a real exchange moment to one product/landing-page decision.

I1

“Don’t turn an introduction into setup.”

Context: a sales rep, freelancer or professional is already talking to someone. App install, login or a long form interrupts that moment.

Design response: show “No app required” early, then explain Create → Share → Save.

I2

“If I have to retype it, I can mistype it.”

Context: paper cards, handwriting and manual entry create errors and slow follow-up.

Design response: make Accurate / exact contact transfer a concrete product proof, not a generic icon.

I3

“A connection is only useful if I can find it later.”

Context: names, social handles, screenshots and paper cards are easy to lose across channels.

Design response: combine identity + contact + social links, and end the recipient flow with Save Contact.

I4

“A better business card should also remove paper waste.”

Brief signal: “Help save 173 trees a day” is a memorable, measurable marketing claim supplied by the brief.

Design response: keep the number visible in the benefit card instead of diluting it into generic sustainability copy.

Why I added marketing sections beyond the brief

Each extra section resolves a specific question rather than adding visual volume.

Evidence discipline: the pain points above are hypotheses until validated. The “173 trees a day” wording is preserved because it is an explicit brief-provided marketing claim, not because I independently verified the environmental calculation.
