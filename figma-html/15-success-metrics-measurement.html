How I would know the design is working

I would measure the full path from understanding the proposition to successfully saving a contact.

Because this is a design test and not a launched product, I do not claim performance numbers. Instead, I define the measurement model, events and failure signals I would use after launch or during validation.

Each stage answers a different question: understand → act → complete → receive value.

Metrics I would track

Behavioral metrics are paired with comprehension and quality signals so conversion alone does not hide UX problems.

MetricWhat it tells meHow I would capture itDesign question
5-second comprehensionWhether the proposition is understood before deep reading.Moderated/unmoderated first-impression test.Does the hero explain product + value clearly?
Primary CTA click-throughWhether interest translates into intent.Analytics event on Early Access CTA.Is the action visible and compelling?
Signup completion / abandonmentWhether the form introduces avoidable friction.Form-start, error and submit-success events.Are fields, errors and expectations clear?
Recipient save-contact completionWhether the exchange delivers its promised outcome.Profile-open → save-action → save-success funnel.Does “no app required” hold up in practice?
Time to understand / time on taskHow much effort is required to grasp or complete the flow.Usability test timing + session analytics.Where is cognitive or interaction friction concentrated?
Form error rateWhether users are blocked by input/validation design.Field-level validation event logging.Which inputs need clearer copy or constraints?

No fabricated targets: I would set numeric targets only after baseline data or research benchmarks exist. Until then, the responsible artifact is the measurement plan, not invented percentages.
